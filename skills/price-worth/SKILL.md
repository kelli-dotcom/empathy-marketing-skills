# Price Worth Skill

## Summary
A reasonable price comes from value, not from cost alone. Look at what others charge for the same kind of thing, what people can pay right now, what the product will be worth as it grows, and what the company and the product are actually worth. Then set a price that is fair to the buyer and fair to the owner, and show the reasoning so both sides can see why it is fair.

## The rules
1. Never price from cost alone. Cost is a floor, not a price. The price comes from value: what the buyer gets, and what the thing is worth.
2. Check the marketplace first. Find what others charge for the same kind of thing. Note the range, the middle, and what the top and bottom include. A price far outside the range needs a reason the buyer can see.
3. Check what people can pay now. The economy sets the ceiling. A price the buyer cannot pay is not a price, it is a wall. Look at what the buyer's audience earns, spends, and already pays for.
4. Weigh projected growth. A product that grows is worth more than a product that stands still. Price for what the product will be worth, not only what it is today. Say how the value grows, in plain words.
5. State the value, then the price. The buyer must see the value before they see the number. If the value is not clear, the price will always look too high.
6. Show the reasoning. Write down each input — marketplace range, what people can pay, projected growth, company and product value — and how they led to the price. If the reasoning cannot be written down, the price is a guess.
7. The price must be fair to the buyer and fair to the owner. Too high and the buyer walks away. Too low and the owner cannot keep going. A fair price is one both sides can stand behind.

## What to measure
- Marketplace: three to five comparable products or services, their prices, and what each includes. The range and the middle.
- Economy: what the buyer's audience can pay right now. What they already pay for similar things.
- Projected growth: how the product's value grows over time — more features, more users, more proof, more reach. What it will be worth in a year if the work continues.
- Company value: what the company behind the product is worth — its reputation, its audience, its track record, its finished work.
- Product value: what the product itself is worth — what it does, what it saves the buyer (time, money, work), and what it would cost the buyer to build or get it another way.

## Run it
1. List the marketplace comparables. Write the range and the middle.
2. Write what the buyer's audience can pay now.
3. Write how the value grows, and what the product will be worth as it grows.
4. Write the company value and the product value in plain words.
5. Set the price inside the marketplace range, within what people can pay, and high enough to be fair to the owner.
6. Write the reasoning down, one line per input, so the owner can see why the price is fair.
7. Check both sides. Would the buyer pay it and feel it was fair? Would the owner take it and keep going? If either answer is no, adjust.
