# Funnel Skill

## Summary
Turn free users into paid buyers with a funnel built on tested evidence, not guesswork. Map the stages, measure each one, fix the biggest leak first, and test one change at a time.

## The stages
Use AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action — first described by E. St. Elmo Lewis in 1898 and still the foundation of funnel thinking. For a solo seller, map it to four plain stages:
1. See — a stranger finds the free thing (a repo, a post, a theme, a skill).
2. Try — they use the free thing.
3. Want — they see the paid thing solves a problem the free thing does not.
4. Buy — they pay.

Measure the conversion rate between each stage. The funnel is a diagnostic tool: find the leak, fix it. Do not redesign the whole funnel when one stage is leaking.

## What good looks like (benchmarks)
B2B SaaS benchmarks from First Page Sage (client data, about 65% B2B):
- Visitor to lead: 2.3% average. Above 10% is strong. Below 1% is broken.
- Lead to MQL: 39%. MQL to SQL: 38%. SQL to opportunity: 42%. Opportunity to close: 37%.
- VWO ranges: visitor to lead 1–5%, lead to MQL 25–35%, MQL to SQL 13–26%, SQL to opportunity 50–62%, opportunity to close 15–30%.
- Median landing page conversion across all industries: 6.6% (41,000+ landing pages, 464 million visitors, Unbounce).
- Overall end-to-end funnel conversion: 3–10%.

Free to paid (Poyar / ChartMogul / ProductLed 2026, 200 B2B products):
- Free trial, no card required: about 18% convert to paid (First Page Sage). Good is 4–6%, great is 10–15% (ChartMogul).
- Free trial, card required: about 49% convert. Good is 25–35%, great is 50–60%.
- Freemium: 2.6–3.7% convert. Good is 3–5%, great is 6–8%.
- Median free-to-paid across all products: 8%.
- Freemium brings about 90 signups per 1,000 visits against about 45 for a free trial, so a lower rate can still produce more customers end to end.

## Tested tactics that lift conversion
Each tactic below was measured in a named test or study. Use them in order.
1. Cut form fields. Every extra field costs 5–10% conversion. Cutting an 11-field form to 4 fields lifted conversion from 12% to 31% (+158%, Source Orthopedic, 5Kings CRO playbook, 100+ tests). Ask only for what a decision needs.
2. Say the value above the fold. The headline pattern that won 90% of B2B landing page tests: [specific outcome] for [specific audience] in [timeframe] (5Kings).
3. Show social proof above the fold. It wins 80% of the time. The format that works: 3 customer logos + 1 sentence of testimony + one number (5Kings). Cialdini's field tests back it: a card saying most guests reuse towels lifted reuse from 35.1% to 44.1%; a card citing guests who stayed in the same room reached 49.3% (Goldstein, Cialdini & Griskevicius, 2008). Proof works best from people like the buyer.
4. Show the price. Hiding pricing loses 20–40% against showing it, even as a range (5Kings).
5. Cut checkout steps. A four-step checkout collapsed to one page gave a median 11% lift (Stripe, 12,000 merchants). A four-step checkout collapsed to two steps lifted conversions 35% (furniture retailer case, StrategyC). A five-step checkout abandons 25–40% more carts than a single-page one (Baymard).
6. Answer fast. Leads contacted within an hour are nearly 7 times more likely to qualify than leads contacted later (Harvard Business Review, Oldroyd et al., 2011, 2,241 firms).
7. Give first. Enclosing free personalised address labels in a donation request doubled the response rate (Cialdini, Harvard Business Review). The free repo, theme, or skill is the gift; the paid product is the ask that follows.
8. Reassure at the decision point. A reassurance message at the passenger step lifted conversion 25.18% (Omniconvert, Tripsta). Say what happens after they pay, and what happens if it does not work.

## Mistakes that kill conversion
From Baymard Institute (average cart abandonment 70.19% across 49 studies):
- Hidden costs. 48% of shoppers cite extra costs as the reason they abandoned — the top reason since 2017. Show the total price early.
- Forced account creation. 26% abandoned for this reason alone. Let the buyer buy as a guest; offer the account after.
- Too many fields. The average checkout has about 23 form fields; about 12 are needed. Each unnecessary field is a chance to leave.
- Multi-step forms for a short job. 81% of people who start a multi-step form abandon it. Single-page forms complete at 53% against 13.85% for multi-page (IvyForms analysis of Baymard and Zuko data).
- No follow-up. Slow replies discard completions the funnel fought to win (see tactic 6).

## How the free-to-paid funnel works
1. Free at the top. The free repos, themes, and skills are the top of the funnel. They cost the buyer nothing and prove the work is real.
2. The paid thing must solve a problem the free thing does not. Free is the method and the engine. Paid is the finished, tested, ready-to-ship work. If the paid thing is just more of the free thing, nobody buys.
3. Pick the model by the numbers, not by preference. A free trial converts a higher share but brings fewer people; freemium brings about twice the signups at a much lower rate. Work the math end to end: visits × signup rate × paid rate = customers.
4. Track product-qualified leads. Only about 24–25% of product-led companies track them, but PQL users convert about 3 times better, and tracking them raised the chance of fast growth by 61% (OpenView 2023, ProductLed 2025). A PQL is a free user who has done the thing the paid product does — that is the moment to make the offer.
5. One call to action per step. One page, one job, one button. Every extra choice is a leak.

## Where the funnel converts
The sales agent is the conversion point. The funnel brings a free user to Want; the sales agent closes it. The sales agent checks the skill registry, recommends a repo and a skill the owner already has (paid or free, price stated), or offers a custom skill or product made for the buyer. The transaction is ethical: price upfront, approve before paying, the buyer owns what they buy, refund if it does not work. The voice is the empathy skills: user-empathy-research (frame the recommendation around what the person needs) and category-positioning-audit (say who it is for and who it is not for), written in the owner's human voice. The funnel never sells. It brings the person to the agent, and the agent helps them.

## Run it
1. Draw the four stages. Write the current count at each stage.
2. Find the biggest leak (the lowest conversion against the benchmarks above).
3. Fix that leak first, with one tactic from the list.
4. Test one change at a time. A test needs enough conversions to be valid (about 1,000 per variant for a real read, 5Kings).
5. Keep what wins. Write the result down. Repeat.
